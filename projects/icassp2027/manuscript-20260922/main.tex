\begin{abstract}
Detecting rare events requires sustained observation, but not necessarily full computation for every internal video token. We introduce PairSelect, a training-free token selection operator for frozen weakly supervised video anomaly detectors. After dense contextualization in the first half of a video encoder, PairSelect allocates local quotas and retains activation-magnitude-guided representatives without averaging their vectors. It uses adjacent spatial pairs for joint-attention encoders and complete spatial trajectories for divided space--time attention. Neither input clip coverage nor the dense-trained detection head is changed. Across three encoders and two datasets, the completed middle-budget evaluation removes approximately 40--43\% of subsequent tokens, with primary-metric changes from $-0.16$ to $+0.68$ percentage points. Analytical accounting predicts 21.75--23.04\% fewer operations in the twelve principal transformer blocks. Additional budgets and measurements of full-encoder FLOPs, latency, throughput, and peak GPU memory remain to be completed.
\end{abstract}
