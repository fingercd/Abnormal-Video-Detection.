\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
\textbf{Weakly supervised anomaly detection.}
Multiple-instance ranking~\cite{sultani2018real}, robust temporal feature magnitudes~\cite{tian2021rtfm}, and uncertainty-regulated dual memories~\cite{zhou2023urdmu} address video-level supervision. VadCLIP adapts frozen vision--language representations through a task-specific detection architecture~\cite{wu2024vadclip}. Efficient anomaly detectors also include learned autoencoder designs~\cite{ristea2024aedae}. We use UR-DMU as a fixed downstream head within each encoder--dataset configuration, and reduce visual encoding cost rather than introduce another detector. Our setup is visual-only even on the audiovisual XD-Violence benchmark~\cite{wu2020xd}.

\textbf{Efficient transformers.}
VideoMAE and VideoMAEv2 use masked video pretraining~\cite{tong2022videomae,wang2023videomaev2}; TimeSformer separates temporal and spatial attention~\cite{bertasius2021timesformer}. DynamicViT learns token-importance predictors~\cite{rao2021dynamicvit}, and EViT reorganizes attentive and inattentive tokens~\cite{liang2022evit}. EVAD studies task-aware token dropout for action detection~\cite{chen2023evad}. ATS provides parameter-free sampling, and ToMe supports merging without additional training~\cite{fayyaz2022ats,bolya2023tome}. Thus, training-free reduction itself is not new. \method{} studies a local non-mixing rule at a fixed intermediate depth with an unchanged anomaly head and unchanged clip coverage. ESOM and LAVIDA instead compress tokens in multimodal language-model pipelines~\cite{liu2026esom,dai2026lavida}, unlike our frozen visual-encoder/MIL setting.
