\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Video anomaly detection faces an asymmetric computational problem: unusual events are scarce, yet a system must process long stretches of ordinary activity to find them. In weakly supervised video anomaly detection (WSVAD), video-level training labels do not identify the anomalous snippets, which can be overwhelmed by normal instances~\cite{sultani2018real,tian2021rtfm}. A practical pipeline extracts clip representations with a pretrained encoder and passes the resulting sequence to a temporal detector~\cite{zhou2023urdmu}. Reducing the number of input clips is one way to lower cost, but it also changes the temporal observations available to that detector. We instead ask how to reduce internal computation while keeping the original clip schedule intact.

\emph{The rarity of anomalous events does not imply that every internal token carries unique information.} VideoMAE demonstrates the usefulness of video redundancy for masked pretraining~\cite{tong2022videomae}; token sampling and merging also exploit redundant representations in pretrained transformers~\cite{fayyaz2022ats,bolya2023tome}. These findings motivate a different allocation of inference effort: allow every token to participate in the first half of the encoder, then continue processing only a structured subset. Pretraining redundancy alone does not establish that a frozen anomaly detector tolerates this intervention. The resulting representations must still work with its existing detection head.

We introduce \method{}, a local, non-mixing selection operator for this setting (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}). It combines three choices: spatially adjacent candidate units, fixed group budgets, and an activation-magnitude preference within those units. For joint-attention encoders, a first selection round gives each pair a representative whenever the budget permits; remaining slots retain additional members of stronger pairs. For divided space--time attention, entire spatial trajectories are selected together. The selected vectors are gathered without averaging at the insertion point. No selector is trained, no compression-specific labels are required, and neither the encoder nor the dense-trained head is fine-tuned after insertion.

Our contributions are a training-free, structure-aware selection rule for frozen WSVAD pipelines; explicit budget and computational-cost accounting for joint and divided attention; and a direct-insertion evaluation on three encoders and two datasets. At the completed middle budget, the largest observed primary-metric decrease is 0.16 percentage points, while approximately 40--43\% of tokens are removed from the remaining encoder blocks. The budget ladder separates local duplicate removal from more aggressive loss of local representatives, providing a concrete basis for studying quality--cost trade-offs rather than assuming a preferred compression ratio.
