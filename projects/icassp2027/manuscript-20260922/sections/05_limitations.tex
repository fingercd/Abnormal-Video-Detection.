\section{Scope and Discussion}
\label{sec:limitations}
The evidence covers three backbones from two principal attention families and one downstream detector family. A shared selection rule does not imply identical behavior across architectures, datasets, or metrics. Mid-depth insertion and the group size are fixed design choices, not proven optima. The current controls do not substitute for a direct comparison with a standard external reducer such as ATS or ToMe. Earlier engineering diagnostics accessed UCF test data; the study is therefore not presented as wholly test-blind development. Additional budgets must be reported together rather than selected post hoc to imply an independently validated optimum.

The local-coverage rule constrains where token removal occurs, but it does not identify abnormal locations or guarantee preservation of short events. Dynamic anchor positions are an implementation approximation. The method reduces internal activation processing, not model weights, output feature dimension, or the number of detector inputs. Its deployment benefit ultimately depends on measured arithmetic cost, execution overhead, and hardware behavior together.
