\subsection{Frozen pipeline and design rationale}
Let $E_{1:b}$ and $E_{b+1:L}$ denote the prefix and suffix of a pretrained encoder, $R$ its clip readout, and $H$ the temporal detection head. For scheduled clips $\{v_t\}_{t=1}^{M}$, inference is
\begin{equation}
 z_t^{(r)}=R\!\left(E_{b+1:L}\!\left(S_r(E_{1:b}(v_t))\right)\right),
 \quad \hat{\boldsymbol y}^{(r)}=H(\{z_t^{(r)}\}_{t=1}^{M}).
 \label{eq:pipeline}
\end{equation}
Only $S_r$, the selection operator at retention budget $r$, is inserted. We set $L=12$ and $b=6$ for all three encoders. The clip count $M$, output dimension, and head weights remain unchanged. Training-free refers to compression: the original detection head is trained once on dense features for each configuration.

Local redundancy motivates nearby comparisons; gathering existing vectors avoids additional mixing before the unchanged head. ATS motivates inexpensive magnitude-based scoring~\cite{fayyaz2022ats}, but our block-output L2 norm differs from its attention-weighted value norm. High norm is not an anomaly probability and may occur in background regions~\cite{darcet2024registers}; local quotas therefore constrain magnitude preference rather than treating it as a global anomaly oracle.

\subsection{Local quotas and pair-member priority}
For VideoMAE and VideoMAEv2, the block output is $X\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times D}$ with a known patch grid. We form nonoverlapping horizontal pairs, ordered by native coordinates. Consecutive groups contain at most ten tokens (five pairs). A group with $n_g$ tokens retains
\begin{equation}
 q_g(r)=\left\lfloor rn_g+\tfrac12\right\rfloor,
 \qquad r\in\{0.8,0.6,0.4\}.
 \label{eq:quota}
\end{equation}
For pair $j$ with members $(x_{j,0},x_{j,1})$, define
\begin{equation}
 a_j=\arg\max_{u\in\{0,1\}}\norm{x_{j,u}},\qquad
 s_j=\max_{u\in\{0,1\}}\norm{x_{j,u}}.
 \label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
Ties retain native order. Let $w_j=x_{j,a_j}$ and $\ell_j=x_{j,1-a_j}$. If $\pi$ sorts the $m_g$ pairs by descending $s_j$, the group's priority list is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal{P}_g=
 (w_{\pi_1},\ldots,w_{\pi_{m_g}},\ell_{\pi_1},\ldots,\ell_{\pi_{m_g}}).
 \label{eq:priority}
\end{equation}
We take the first $q_g$ entries, then restore the selected indices to native order before gathering. At $r=0.8$ or $0.6$, every pair in a full group retains a representative; at $r=0.4$, only four of its five representatives survive. This is a coverage property of the rule, not a guarantee about anomaly preservation. No averaging or rescaling is applied to a selected vector.

\subsection{Divided attention and actual budgets}
TimeSformer requires a different unit: all $T$ tokens at a spatial location form a trajectory, scored by the maximum member norm. Each group of ten trajectories retains its top $q_g$ trajectories. The current bridge requires the retained spatial count to be a multiple of the native patch width $W$; we round the total downward and remove excess quota from tail groups. With $P=196$, $T=8$, and $W=14$,
\begin{equation}
 P'=W\left\lfloor\frac{\sum_g q_g(r)}{W}\right\rfloor,
 \qquad K=TP'+1,
 \label{eq:trajectory}
\end{equation}
where the CLS token is preserved. This width constraint belongs to the current implementation, not to all divided-attention models. Dynamic selections occupy a fixed quota-slot skeleton: gathered content is unchanged, but slot coordinates use approximate anchors rather than an exact reconstruction of the original lattice. Complete trajectories preserve their time indices; the geometry adjustment can empty tail groups.



\subsection{Computational accounting}
For joint attention with hidden width $D$ and MLP ratio four, the principal block cost in multiply--accumulate operations (MACs) is
\begin{equation}
 C_{\mathrm J}(N,D)=12ND^2+2N^2D.
 \label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
The linear term includes QKV, output projection, and the MLP; only attention products scale quadratically in $N$. For standard divided attention, including the temporal projection and replicated CLS in spatial attention~\cite{bertasius2021timesformer},
\begin{equation}
 \begin{split}
 C_{\mathrm T}(P,T,D)={}&(17PT+4T+8)D^2\\
 &+2D\{PT^2+T(P+1)^2\}.
 \end{split}
 \label{eq:divided}
\end{equation}
The twelve-block cost ratio is $\tfrac12(1+C_{\rm reduced}/C_{\rm dense})$. Table~\ref{tab:budgets} uses $D=768$ and counts one MAC as two FLOPs. It excludes embedding, readout, normalization, and selection costs. The selector adds norm computation, bounded-size sorting, and gathering; these may have non-negligible runtime despite low arithmetic cost. Token compression, full-encoder FLOPs, latency, and peak memory are therefore distinct quantities.
