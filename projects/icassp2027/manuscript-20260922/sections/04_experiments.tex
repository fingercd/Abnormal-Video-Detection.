\subsection{Setup and evaluation}
We evaluate UCF-Crime~\cite{sultani2018real} with 1,610 training and 290 test videos, and XD-Violence~\cite{wu2020xd} with 3,950 accepted training videos and all 800 test videos. Four of XD's 3,954 declared training videos are excluded after source-integrity checks. Only visual input and video-level training labels are used. Each of VideoMAEv2-B, VideoMAE-B, and TimeSformer-B is paired with a separately trained UR-DMU head~\cite{zhou2023urdmu}. Head training uses 200 temporal bins, 64 normal and 64 anomalous bags per step, Adam with learning rate $10^{-4}$ and weight decay $5\!\times\!10^{-5}$, and the final checkpoint after 3,000 steps. This is a controlled dense-feature training protocol, not a reproduction of the detector's published I3D-feature scores.

The same head checkpoint, preprocessing, and clip schedule are reused across compression settings within a configuration. VideoMAE-family clips yield $8\!\times\!14\!\times\!14=1568$ tokens; TimeSformer yields the same patch count plus CLS. The completed runs preserve 69,364 UCF and 145,703 XD clips per VideoMAE-family encoder, and 138,853 and 291,781 clips for TimeSformer. Clip totals differ across encoders, but dense and reduced paths match exactly within each configuration. Model weights are frozen during compressed inference.

UCF uses frame ROC-AUC. XD uses trapezoidal PR-AUC under the fixed author-style 16-frame prefix alignment; non-interpolated average precision (AP) is reported separately. These are different integration conventions, not interchangeable names. Differences use 10,000 paired video-level bootstrap resamples stratified by video label. The intervals reflect test-video sampling uncertainty conditional on the reported runs, not variability across training seeds. The 20\% and 60\% deletion evaluations are a budget-sensitivity extension; no preferred operating point is inferred from missing results.

\subsection{Direct insertion without head adaptation}
Table~\ref{tab:quality} reports the completed middle-budget results. On UCF, \method{} changes ROC-AUC by $+0.39$, $-0.16$, and approximately $0.00$ points for VideoMAEv2, VideoMAE, and TimeSformer, respectively. On XD, the corresponding trapezoidal PR-AUC changes are $+0.32$, $+0.61$, and $+0.68$ points. These point estimates support the feasibility of middle-layer reduction with an unchanged head across the tested encoders. They do not imply a universal improvement: all three UCF intervals include both positive and negative changes.

The alternate XD metric matters. Non-interpolated AP changes from 70.33 to 71.30 for VideoMAEv2, 64.86 to 64.51 for VideoMAE, and 70.86 to 72.04 for TimeSformer. In particular, VideoMAE improves under trapezoidal PR-AUC but decreases under AP. We retain both definitions rather than interpret every positive primary-metric change as an unqualified detection gain.



\subsection{Budget behavior and reference selections}
The 20\%, 40\%, and 60\% deletion settings retain the same insertion depth. For joint attention, the highest setting drops entire pair representatives: a possible explanation for quality changes, not an established breakpoint. TimeSformer's actual highest deletion is 64.24\%; rounding must be reported but is not an isolated causal explanation.

Table~\ref{tab:references} places the current method alongside fixed-prefix and fixed-seed random selection within each group. Neither is a global uniform sampler, and random masks are reused across videos. Rankings vary: random is higher on all three UCF configurations, whereas \method{} is higher on two XD configurations. Thus these references do not establish universal superiority of the norm signal. Their static native-position gathering also differs from the dynamic anchor-slot assignment, so this is a matched-budget reference rather than an isolated norm-only ablation.

\subsection{FLOPs and hardware efficiency}

Table~\ref{tab:efficiency} separates analytical and unmeasured hardware quantities. Principal-block FLOPs decrease by 23.04\% (joint attention) and 21.75\% (divided attention) at the middle budget, and 33.05\% and 32.43\% at the highest deletion. Savings are smaller than token deletion because the first six blocks remain dense.

Measurements will fix GPU, input shape, batch size, precision, and attention backend. Full-encoder timing includes embedding, all blocks, selection, gathering, and readout; only diagnostic CPU exports are disabled. The protocol uses 20 warm-up passes, synchronized CUDA events, and three independent process repeats on identical representative non-test clips. Report throughput at the stated batch, batch-one latency, and peak allocated/reserved memory after synchronized counter resets, together with hardware and software identifiers.

Decoding, host--device transfer, feature writing, and head inference require separate synchronized wall-clock timing. Different clip schedules preclude an end-to-end cross-encoder ranking by clips/s. The dense prefix or selector temporaries may determine peak memory; analytical savings establish neither measured speedup nor memory reduction.
